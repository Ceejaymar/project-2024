# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers and recruiters scanning a shortlist. They arrive with limited time and a clear question: "Is this person the right hire?" They are not reading every word; they are forming an impression. The portfolio must signal quality, creativity, and reliability before they consciously decide to keep scrolling.

Secondary audience: fellow engineers and technical leads who dig deeper into stack choices, code quality, and project decisions once the initial impression passes.

## Product Purpose

Carlos Martinez's personal portfolio. A Frontend Engineer's public proof of work. The site exists to move someone from "interesting candidate" to "I want to talk to this person." Success looks like a recruiter forwarding it with a note, or a technical lead clicking through to a project and staying.

## Brand Personality

Calm, crafted, intentional. Nothing is there by accident. The personality is quiet confidence: someone who has a strong point of view but doesn't need to announce it. The interface itself demonstrates the claim "Frontend Engineer" without saying so twice.

## Anti-references

- Generic dev-portfolio templates: identical card grids, cookie-cutter hero sections with typing animations.
- Loud, overwrought designs: neon-on-black, excessive gradient text, animations that exist to show off rather than guide.
- AI-default aesthetics: purple gradients on white, Space Grotesk everywhere, hero metrics, glassmorphism as decoration.
- Corporate blandness: safe, safe, safe — indistinguishable from a resume in HTML.

## Design Principles

1. **Craft is the argument.** The portfolio is itself a frontend project. Its quality is the proof. Every interaction, spacing decision, and animation carries an implicit claim.
2. **Inspire before you inform.** Recruiters scan; engineers dig. The first layer must evoke something — curiosity, confidence, warmth — before the second layer delivers facts.
3. **Calm over flash.** Restraint is harder than exuberance. Prefer one well-placed detail over three competing ones. The quieter choice usually wins.
4. **Hierarchy does the work.** A recruiter gives 10 seconds. What they see in those 10 seconds is the design's job, not copy's. Layout and scale carry meaning that words cannot.
5. **Nothing is filler.** Every section, line of copy, and component must justify its space. If it can be removed without loss, it should be.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Both light and dark mode supported. Reduced-motion preferences respected via `prefers-reduced-motion` (already implemented in GlobalStyles). Semantic HTML, visible focus states, and descriptive alt text are baseline requirements.
